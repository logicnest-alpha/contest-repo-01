# Client Acquisition Playbook: How to Get Your First Clients

Companion to the reel *How to Get Your First Clients*. This is the system I'm learning and testing
as a student founder. It isn't a results report. Real numbers will come in future episodes, good or bad.

**The whole idea in one line:** your first clients come from conversations, not from posting more.

```
pick one customer + one problem → list 100 people → start warm → show, don't pitch
→ call: ask more than you talk → small paid pilot → deliver → testimonial + "who else?" → repeat
```

---

## Step 1. One customer, one painful problem

"I build websites" is a service. Nobody wakes up wanting one.
"I help dentists fill empty appointment slots" is a **problem solved for a specific person**.

**Your one-sentence offer:**
> I help **[specific type of customer]** **[get a specific outcome]** without **[the thing they hate]**.

Examples:
- I help **coaching institutes** get **more demo-class sign-ups** without **running expensive ads**.
- I help **cafés** take **direct online orders** without **paying delivery-app commissions**.
- I help **gyms** win back **members who stopped coming** without **cold-calling them**.

**Pick a niche where all four are true:**
1. **The problem is painful and visible.** You can see it from outside: an outdated site, no reviews, a slow booking flow, a job post.
2. **They can pay.** They're a business with revenue, not a hobby.
3. **You can reach them.** You know some of them, or can find them on Google Maps, LinkedIn or Instagram.
4. **You can actually solve it**, or learn to quickly. Don't sell what you can't deliver.

---

## Step 2. Write down 100 people who have that problem

Use a spreadsheet:
`name · business · how I know them (warm/cold) · where (IG/LinkedIn/email/phone) · the problem I noticed ·
status (to contact / messaged / replied / call / pilot / client / no) · next step · date`

**Start warm.** You know more people than you think:
- friends, family, relatives
- classmates, seniors, alumni, teachers, professors
- anyone you've worked with, interned with, or helped before
- local businesses you already go to

**Then go cold:** Google Maps (search "[niche] near [city]"), LinkedIn, Instagram, industry
directories, job boards (a hiring post = a visible need). For cold email, follow the Episode 004
cold email playbook (`Animated_Channel_EP004/Cold_Email_Playbook.md`).

### Warm message scripts

**Referral ask (best first message):**
> Hey [name]! Random question: I've started helping [type of business] with [problem].
> Do you know anyone who runs a [type of business]? Even one name would really help.

**Direct, to someone who owns a relevant business:**
> Hey [name], I noticed [specific, true observation about their business].
> I've been learning how to fix exactly that. Mind if I record a 2-minute video showing two things
> I'd change? Free, no strings.

## Step 3. Don't pitch. Show.

A pitch asks them to imagine the value. A **2-minute video** shows it to them.

**The 2-minute video (Loom or a screen recording):**
| Time | What to say and show |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | "Hi [name], I'm [you]. I made this specifically for [business] because I noticed [observation]." |
| 0:15–1:30 | **Two concrete fixes**, shown on *their* actual website, page or profile. Why each one matters for them. |
| 1:30–2:00 | "If you want, I can do this for you. Reply 'yes' and I'll send a simple plan." |

**Message to send with it:**
> Hi [name], I recorded a 2-minute video with two things I'd change on [their page] to help with [problem]:
> [link]
> No pitch, happy to just leave it with you. If it's useful, reply "yes" and I'll send a simple plan.

Rules: one video per business, made for them. No templates with names swapped. Two fixes, not ten.
Real and specific beats impressive.

---

## Step 4. The call: ask more than you talk

Aim for **them 70%, you 30%**. You're diagnosing, not presenting.

**Call flow (20–30 min):**
1. **Open (2 min):** "Thanks for the time. I'd like to understand how things work today, then tell you
   honestly whether I can help. Sound good?"
2. **Situation:** "Walk me through how you handle [area] today." "Who does it?"
3. **Pain (the three questions):**
   - "What is this costing you, in time, money or stress?"
   - "What have you tried so far?"
   - "What happens if nothing changes in the next 6 months?"
4. **Priority:** "Out of everything on your plate, where does this rank?"
5. **Decision:** "How do you usually decide on things like this? Is anyone else involved?"
6. **Close:** summarize their problem in their words, then propose a small paid pilot (Step 5).

If you can't help, say so and point them to someone who can. That honesty earns referrals.

---

## Step 5. Offer a small, paid pilot

Not "free forever." Not a giant retainer. A **small, paid, time-boxed pilot**:
**one outcome · one price · two weeks.** Low risk for them, proof for you.

**One-page pilot proposal:**
> **Pilot: [outcome] for [business]**
> **The problem:** [their words from the call]
> **What I'll do:** [3 bullet deliverables]
> **How we'll know it worked:** [one measurable thing, e.g. booking form live and tested, X reviews requested]
> **Timeline:** 2 weeks, starting [date]
> **Price:** [one fixed price], paid [upfront / 50% now, 50% on delivery]
> **Not included:** [what's out of scope]
> **After the pilot:** if it works, we can talk about [ongoing option]. No obligation.

**Pricing:** pick one fixed price that's an easy yes for a pilot, but real money, so both sides take it
seriously. Don't charge by the hour, and don't do unlimited unpaid work. Only promise outcomes you
control. Promise deliverables, not "2× revenue."

---

## Step 6. Deliver, then ask for proof and referrals

- **Over-communicate:** a short update every 2–3 days, even when there's not much to report.
- **Finish early** if you can, and recap what changed (before/after screenshots).

**Testimonial ask:**
> Really enjoyed working on this. Would you be open to sharing 2–3 lines about what it was like
> and what changed? Honest is better than polished.

**Referral ask ("who else?"):**
> One more thing: do you know one or two other [business owners] who deal with the same problem?
> A quick intro would mean a lot.

Never write or edit a testimonial yourself, and never invent one.

---

## Objections: honest answers

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "It's too expensive." | "Fair. What would make it clearly worth it to you?" Then shrink the scope, not the price. |
| "Send me more info." | "Sure. So I send the right thing, what's the main thing you'd want to know?" |
| "We already have someone." | "Great. Is [problem] fully handled, or is there a gap I could look at?" |
| "Not right now." | "Totally fine. When would be a better time to check back?" Note it and follow up then. |
| "Why should I trust a student?" | "Honestly, you shouldn't take my word for it. That's why I start with a small, fixed-scope pilot, so you can judge the work itself." |

## The daily system

**10 new conversations a day**, every day: warm messages, cold emails, DMs, referral asks, follow-ups.

| Track weekly | Why |
|---|---|
| Conversations started | Effort you control |
| Replies | Is your message and targeting working? |
| Calls booked | Is your offer interesting? |
| Pilots sold | Is the offer clear and low-risk enough? |
| Delivered + testimonials + referrals | Is the loop turning? |

If a number is low, fix **the step right before it**. Change one thing at a time.

---

## 30-day plan

| Days | Do |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Write your one-sentence offer. Pick the niche using the 4 checks |
| 3–5 | Build the 100-person list: warm first, then cold |
| 6–7 | Send the referral ask to everyone in your warm network |
| 8–30 | 10 conversations a day. Record 2-minute videos for the 3–5 best-fit businesses each week |
| Ongoing | Every call → pilot offer. Every pilot → testimonial + "who else?" |
| Every Sunday | Review the five numbers. Change one thing. Document it honestly |

---

## Ethics
- No fake testimonials, inflated results, or fake scarcity.
- Promise what you control (deliverables, timeline), not outcomes you don't (revenue).
- If someone says no or stop, respect it immediately.
